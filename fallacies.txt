Strawman: misrepresenting argument
Slippery slope: weed is a gateway drug to heroin, so we should ban weed
Ad hominem: personal attack of opponent
Tu quoque: answering criticism with criticism
Incredulity: dismissing argument because you find it hard to understand
Special pleading: making exceptions when claim is refuted
Double standard
Burden of proof: make opponent find evidence for your claim
Ambiguity: using double meanings or unclear definitions
Bandwagon: appeal to popularity
Appeal to authority: Experts say so
Composition/division: Some protestors were violent, so all protestors are violent
False dichotomy: present only two options, when other possibilities exist
Begging the question/circular reasoning/assuming the conclusion
Appeal to nature: because something is "natural", it is good
Anecdotal evidence: use personal experience to dismiss hard data
Texas sharpshooter: cherry-pick evidence
Middle ground: the truth must be a compromise between opposing positions
Motte-and-Bailey: make strong claim, substitute weaker claim when challenged
Red herring: distract by raising an irrelevant issue
